Make it easy to understand and answer your request.

A referral can improve visibility, but it does not replace role alignment or interview performance. Good outreach is specific, truthful, respectful and easy to decline.

Would you refer this person?

“Hi, I saw you work at Company X. Please refer me for any ML openings.” What makes this difficult to answer?

The target role is missing, there is no relevant evidence, the requester has not shown alignment, and “any opening” transfers the search work to the recipient. A referral also asks someone to associate their reputation with a candidate they may not know.

What a referral actually does

May improve visibility

An application may enter an employee referral channel or receive contextual attention.

Does not guarantee selection

The candidate still needs suitable evidence and must clear the interview.

Requires comfort

A thoughtful request allows the person to review the resume and decline without pressure.

Referral request structure

Name the exact role

Include the role title and link or requisition ID.

Show two pieces of relevant evidence

Use outcomes or ownership, not an autobiography.

Explain the connection

Why are you approaching this person rather than sending the same message to everyone?

Make a small, respectful request

Ask them to consider referring after reviewing the evidence. Give them room to decline.

Hi Priya, I am applying for the Machine Learning Engineer role on the recommendations team [link]. My recent work includes deploying a ranking model that improved top-10 recall by 11% and building its feature pipeline across 4.2M interactions. If, after reviewing my resume, you feel comfortable referring me, I would be grateful. No worries if you do not know my work well enough. I would still appreciate any insight about the team. Thanks, [Name]

Cold outreach is usually a conversation request

Too broad

“Can you guide me about getting into machine learning?”

Focused

“Your move from analytics into ML platform work caught my attention. I am preparing for a similar transition. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about how your team evaluates production ML depth? I will come with two focused questions.”

Follow-up etiquette

  • Send one concise follow-up after a reasonable interval.
  • Add useful context rather than writing only “following up.”
  • Do not pressure someone to refer without reviewing your background.
  • Thank people who respond, even when they cannot help.
  • Track outreach so the same person does not receive repeated generic messages.
